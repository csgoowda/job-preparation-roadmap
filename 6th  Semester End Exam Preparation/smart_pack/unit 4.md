# Introduction

Packaging materials such as **plastic, paper, glass, and metals** generate large amounts of waste after use. A **recycling strategy** is a systematic approach to **collecting, sorting, processing, and reusing packaging materials** to reduce environmental pollution, conserve natural resources, and promote sustainable development.

---

# Objectives of Recycling

* Reduce packaging waste
* Conserve natural resources
* Save energy
* Reduce environmental pollution
* Promote sustainable development
* Minimize landfill waste
* Encourage reuse of materials

* Recycling centres

* Metal

### Step 6 – Reuse

# Introduction

Modern food packaging is not limited to protecting products. **Smart packaging** improves food quality, safety, and shelf life.

Smart packaging is mainly classified into:

1. **Active Packaging**
2. **Intelligent Packaging**

Active packaging **interacts with the product**, while intelligent packaging **monitors and provides information about the product**.

---

# 1. Active Packaging

## Definition

**Active packaging** is a packaging system that **actively interacts with the food or its surrounding environment** to maintain freshness, improve safety, and extend shelf life.

Unlike conventional packaging, active packaging changes the internal conditions of the package to preserve food quality. 

---

## Working Principle

Active packaging controls the package atmosphere by:

* Absorbing unwanted substances
* Releasing beneficial substances

This slows spoilage and maintains food quality.

---

## Types of Active Packaging

### 1. Oxygen Scavengers

* Remove oxygen from the package.
* Prevent oxidation and microbial growth.

**Example:** Meat and bakery products.

---

### 2. Moisture Absorbers

* Remove excess moisture.
* Prevent mold growth.

**Example:** Biscuit and snack packets.

---

### 3. Ethylene Absorbers

* Remove ethylene gas.
* Delay ripening of fruits and vegetables.

**Example:** Banana and apple packaging.

---

### 4. Carbon Dioxide Emitters

* Release CO₂ to inhibit microbial growth.

**Example:** Fresh meat packaging.

---

### 5. Antimicrobial Packaging

* Releases antimicrobial agents.
* Reduces bacterial growth.

**Example:** Cheese and seafood packaging.

---

# Advantages of Active Packaging

* Extends shelf life
* Maintains freshness
* Improves food safety
* Reduces food waste
* Controls moisture and oxygen
* Improves product quality

---

# Applications

* Meat
* Fish
* Bakery products
* Fruits
* Vegetables
* Dairy products

---

# 2. Intelligent Packaging

## Definition

**Intelligent packaging** is a packaging system that **monitors the condition of the packaged product** and provides useful information about its quality, freshness, and storage conditions.

It does not change the food but provides information to manufacturers, retailers, and consumers. 

---

## Working Principle

Intelligent packaging uses sensors and indicators to monitor:

* Temperature
* Freshness
* Time
* Gas concentration
* Leakage

---

## Types of Intelligent Packaging

### 1. Time–Temperature Indicators (TTI)

Shows whether food has been stored at the correct temperature.

---

### 2. Freshness Indicators

Detect spoilage by sensing gases produced during food deterioration.

---

### 3. Gas Indicators

Detect oxygen or carbon dioxide concentration.

---

### 4. RFID Tags

Track products throughout transportation and storage.

---

### 5. QR Codes / Barcodes

Provide product information and improve traceability.

---

# Advantages of Intelligent Packaging

* Monitors food quality
* Improves traceability
* Detects spoilage
* Reduces food waste
* Improves consumer confidence
* Supports inventory management

---

# Applications

* Meat
* Seafood
* Milk
* Pharmaceuticals
* Frozen foods
* High-value food products

---

Active and intelligent packaging are important components of smart packaging technology. **Active packaging** preserves food by interacting with the package environment, whereas **intelligent packaging** monitors food quality using sensors and indicators. # Introduction

**Polymer laminate materials** are made by bonding two or more polymer films to obtain better **strength, barrier properties, durability, and sealing performance**. Before being used for packaging, these laminates must be tested to ensure they can protect the product during **storage, transportation, and handling**.

The suitability of polymer laminates is evaluated through several laboratory tests that assess their **mechanical, barrier, and sealing properties**.

---

# 1. Tensile Strength Test

## Objective

To determine the **maximum force** a laminate can withstand before breaking.

### Procedure

1. Cut the laminate into a standard specimen.
2. Clamp the specimen in a tensile testing machine.
3. Apply tensile force gradually.
4. Record the breaking force and elongation.

### Importance

* Measures mechanical strength.
* Prevents tearing during handling.

### Applications

* Food pouches
* Plastic films
* Flexible packaging

---

# 2. Peel Strength Test

## Objective

To measure the **bonding strength between laminated layers**.

### Procedure

1. Separate the laminated layers slightly.
2. Fix both ends in a peel testing machine.
3. Pull the layers apart at a constant speed.
4. Measure the peel force required.

### Importance

* Ensures layers remain bonded.
* Prevents delamination.

### Applications

* Multilayer food packages
* Pharmaceutical pouches
* Retort pouches

---

# 3. Seal Strength Test

## Objective

To determine the strength of the **heat-sealed joint** in laminated packaging.

### Procedure

1. Prepare a heat-sealed sample.
2. Clamp both ends in the testing machine.
3. Pull until the seal breaks.
4. Record the seal strength.

### Importance

* Prevents leakage.
* Ensures package integrity.

### Applications

* Milk pouches
* Snack packets
* Frozen food packages

---

# 4. Water Vapour Transmission Rate (WVTR) Test

## Objective

To measure the **amount of water vapour passing through the laminate** over a specified time.

### Procedure

1. Mount the laminate in a WVTR testing apparatus.
2. Maintain controlled temperature and humidity.
3. Measure the quantity of water vapour passing through the film.

### Importance

* Evaluates moisture barrier properties.
* Helps extend shelf life.

### Applications

* Biscuit packaging
* Dry food packaging
* Pharmaceutical products

---

# 5. Oxygen/Gas Transmission Rate (OTR/GTR) Test

## Objective

To measure the **amount of oxygen or gas passing through the laminate**.

### Procedure

1. Fix the laminate in an OTR testing instrument.
2. Maintain controlled pressure.
3. Measure the oxygen or gas passing through the film.

### Importance

* Prevents oxidation.
* Preserves food freshness.
* Extends shelf life.

### Applications

* Coffee packaging
* Meat packaging
* Cheese packaging

# Introduction

**Blow molded containers** are widely used for packaging **milk, water, edible oil, beverages, pharmaceuticals, detergents, and chemicals**. Before use, these containers are tested to ensure they have sufficient **strength, leak resistance, dimensional accuracy, and durability** during storage, transportation, and handling.

Various laboratory tests are carried out to evaluate their performance.

---

# 1. Drop Test

## Objective

To determine the ability of a blow molded container to withstand accidental drops during handling and transportation.

### Process

1. Fill the container with water or the specified test material.
2. Seal the container properly.
3. Drop it from a specified height onto a hard surface.
4. Inspect the container for:

   * Cracks
   * Breakage
   * Leakage
   * Seal failure

### Applications

* Water bottles
* Milk bottles
* Chemical containers

---

# 2. Leak Test

## Objective

To check whether the container is **leak-proof** and has proper sealing.

### Process

1. Fill the container with air or water.
2. Apply internal pressure.
3. Immerse it in water or inspect visually.
4. Observe for air bubbles or liquid leakage.

### Applications

* Beverage bottles
* Pharmaceutical bottles
* Chemical containers

---

# 3. Top Load (Compression) Test

## Objective

To evaluate the container's ability to withstand compressive loads during stacking and transportation.

### Process

1. Place the container in a compression testing machine.
2. Apply increasing vertical load.
3. Record the load at which deformation or failure occurs.

### Applications

* PET bottles
* HDPE containers
* Storage containers

---

# 4. Wall Thickness Test

## Objective

To determine whether the wall thickness of the container is **uniform** throughout.

### Process

1. Measure the wall thickness at different locations using a thickness gauge.
2. Compare the values with design specifications.
3. Identify thin or weak areas.

### Applications

* Plastic bottles
* Blow molded containers
* Fuel tanks

---

# 5. Impact Resistance Test

## Objective

To determine the ability of the container to resist sudden impact without breaking.

### Process

1. Place the container in the impact testing machine.
2. Apply a controlled impact using a pendulum or falling weight.
3. Observe for:

   * Cracks
   * Fracture
   * Permanent deformation

### Applications

* Food containers
* Beverage bottles
* Industrial packaging

---

# Introduction

Barrier properties are one of the most important characteristics of packaging materials. **Water Vapour Transmission Rate (WVTR)** and **Gas Transmission Rate (GTR)** tests are used to evaluate the ability of packaging materials to prevent the passage of **water vapour and gases**. These tests help in selecting suitable packaging materials for food, pharmaceutical, and industrial products.

---

# 1. Water Vapour Transmission Rate (WVTR) Test

## Definition

**WVTR (Water Vapour Transmission Rate)** is the amount of **water vapour that passes through a packaging material** over a specified period under controlled temperature and humidity conditions.

It is usually expressed as:

**g/m²/day** (grams per square metre per day)

---

## Objective

* To measure the **moisture barrier property** of packaging materials.
* To determine the rate of water vapour transmission.
* To evaluate the suitability of packaging for moisture-sensitive products.

---

## Principle

Water vapour moves from a region of **high humidity** to a region of **low humidity** through the packaging material. The amount of vapour transmitted is measured under controlled conditions.

---

## Procedure

### Step 1

Mount the packaging film in the **WVTR testing apparatus**.

### Step 2

Maintain the specified **temperature and relative humidity**.

### Step 3

One side of the film is exposed to high humidity, while the other side is kept at low humidity.

### Step 4

Measure the quantity of water vapour passing through the film over a fixed time.

### Step 5

Calculate the WVTR value in **g/m²/day**.

---

## Applications

* Biscuit packaging
* Snack food packets
* Pharmaceutical blister packs
* Milk powder packaging
* Dry food packaging

# 2. Gas Transmission Rate (GTR / OTR) Test

## Definition

**Gas Transmission Rate (GTR)**, commonly measured as **Oxygen Transmission Rate (OTR)**, is the amount of gas (usually oxygen) that passes through a packaging material under specified conditions.

It is generally expressed as:

**cc/m²/day**

---

## Objective

* To determine the **gas barrier property** of packaging materials.
* To measure oxygen permeability.
* To select suitable materials for oxygen-sensitive products.

---

## Principle

Gas molecules move from the side with **higher gas concentration** to the side with **lower gas concentration** through the packaging film. The amount of gas transmitted is measured using a gas transmission tester.

---

## Procedure

### Step 1

Fix the packaging film in the **GTR/OTR testing apparatus**.

### Step 2

Introduce oxygen or another test gas on one side of the film.

### Step 3

Maintain the required temperature and pressure.

### Step 4

Measure the amount of gas passing through the film over a specified time.

### Step 5

Calculate the Gas Transmission Rate (GTR/OTR).

---

## Applications

* Meat packaging
* Cheese packaging
* Coffee packets
* Ready-to-eat foods
* Vacuum-packed foods

Both tests are essential because they help to:

* Evaluate the barrier properties of packaging materials.
* Protect food from moisture and oxygen.
* Prevent spoilage and microbial growth.
* Maintain freshness, flavour, and aroma.
* Increase the shelf life of packaged products.
* Select suitable packaging materials for different foods.
* Reduce food wastage.
* Ensure product quality during storage and transportation.
* Meet national and international packaging quality standards.

| Purpose      | Moisture barrier evaluation | Oxygen barrier evaluation       |
| Used For     | Dry foods, medicines        | Meat, coffee, dairy products    |

# Introduction

**Shelf life** is the period during which a product remains **safe, fresh, and suitable for consumption** while maintaining its desired quality under recommended storage conditions.

Shelf life is influenced by several **intrinsic (product-related)** and **extrinsic (environment-related)** factors. Proper packaging plays an important role in protecting the product from these factors and extending its shelf life.

---

# Factors Affecting Shelf Life

The factors are broadly classified into:

## A. Intrinsic Factors (Internal Factors)

These factors are related to the nature and composition of the product.

### 1. Moisture Content

* High moisture promotes microbial growth.
* Excess moisture reduces shelf life.

**Example:** Bread and fresh fruits spoil quickly.

---

### 2. pH (Acidity)

* Acidic foods generally have a longer shelf life.
* Neutral foods spoil faster due to microbial growth.

**Example:** Pickles last longer than milk.

---

### 3. Fat Content

* Foods rich in fats undergo oxidation.
* Oxidation causes rancidity and unpleasant odour.

**Example:** Oils, butter, and nuts.

---

### 4. Enzyme Activity

* Enzymes cause changes in colour, flavour, and texture.
* They accelerate food deterioration.

**Example:** Browning of apples and bananas.

---

### 5. Nutrient Composition

* Foods rich in proteins and carbohydrates are more susceptible to microbial attack.

**Example:** Milk and meat products.

---

## B. Extrinsic Factors (External Factors)

These depend on the surrounding storage and transportation conditions.

### 1. Temperature

* High temperature speeds up chemical reactions and microbial growth.
* Low temperature slows spoilage.

---

### 2. Relative Humidity

* High humidity increases moisture absorption.
* Encourages mold and bacterial growth.

---

### 3. Oxygen

* Oxygen causes oxidation.
* Leads to colour, flavour, and nutrient loss.

---

### 4. Light

* UV light destroys vitamins.
* Causes fading of colours and oxidation of fats.

---

### 5. Microorganisms

* Bacteria
* Yeasts
* Moulds

These spoil food and reduce shelf life.

---

### 6. Mechanical Damage

* Improper handling may cause crushing, leakage, or breakage.

# Introduction

Packaging materials must prevent moisture from entering or leaving the package to preserve food quality and extend shelf life. **Water Vapour Transmission Rate (WVTR)** is one of the most important tests used to evaluate the **moisture barrier property** of packaging materials.

---

# Definition of WVTR

**WVTR (Water Vapour Transmission Rate)** is the amount of **water vapour that passes through a packaging material** under specified temperature and humidity conditions over a given period.

It is expressed as:

**g/m²/day (grams per square metre per day)**

A **lower WVTR value** indicates a **better moisture barrier**, which helps extend the shelf life of packaged products.

---

# Objective of WVTR Test

* Measure the moisture barrier property.
* Evaluate packaging suitability.
* Prevent moisture gain or loss.
* Improve product shelf life.
* Select appropriate packaging materials.

---

# Method 1 – Desiccant (Dry Cup) Method

## Principle

A **desiccant** (such as silica gel or calcium chloride) is placed inside a test cup. Water vapour from the surrounding humid environment passes through the packaging film and is absorbed by the desiccant. The increase in weight is measured to determine the WVTR.

---

## Procedure

### Step 1

Fill the test cup with a desiccant.

### Step 2

Seal the packaging film over the cup.

### Step 3

Place the cup in a chamber with controlled temperature and high humidity.

### Step 4

Water vapour passes through the film and is absorbed by the desiccant.

### Step 5

Measure the increase in weight over time.

### Step 6

Calculate the WVTR in **g/m²/day**.

---

## Applications

* Biscuit packaging
* Dry food packaging
* Pharmaceutical packaging
* Snack packets

---

```text
 │ (Silica Gel)    │
 └─────────────────┘

 Measure Weight Gain
```

---

# Method 2 – Water (Wet Cup) Method

## Principle

The test cup contains **water**, creating high humidity inside the cup. Water vapour passes through the packaging film to the drier outside environment. The decrease in weight of the cup is measured to calculate the WVTR.

---

## Procedure

### Step 1

Fill the test cup with distilled water.

### Step 2

Seal the packaging film over the cup.

### Step 3

Place the cup in a controlled low-humidity environment.

### Step 4

Water vapour moves through the film.

### Step 5

Measure the decrease in weight over time.

### Step 6

Calculate the WVTR in **g/m²/day**.

---

## Applications

* Plastic films
* Laminated packaging
* Moisture barrier materials
* Food packages

# Conclusion

Advanced packaging materials are transforming the packaging industry by improving product safety, extending shelf life, enhancing traceability, and promoting sustainability. Technologies such as **smart packaging, active packaging, intelligent packaging, biodegradable materials, and nanotechnology** are becoming increasingly important. However, challenges such as high costs, recycling issues, technical complexity, and regulatory compliance must be addressed for their widespread adoption.
