# Introduction

**Screen printing** is a printing technique in which **ink is forced through a fine mesh screen onto the printing surface using a squeegee**. A stencil blocks the non-printing areas so that ink passes only through the required design. It is widely used for printing on **plastic, glass, metal, paper, fabric, and packaging containers**.

---

# Principle

The principle of screen printing is based on **forcing ink through the open areas of a mesh screen** while the blocked areas prevent ink flow. The desired image is transferred onto the substrate by moving a **squeegee** across the screen.

---

# Construction / Components

The main parts of a screen printing machine are:

* Screen frame
* Mesh screen
* Stencil
* Printing ink
* Squeegee
* Printing substrate (paper, plastic, glass, etc.)

---

# Process of Screen Printing

### Step 1 – Screen Preparation

* A fine mesh screen is tightly stretched over a frame.
* The screen is cleaned before use.

---

### Step 2 – Stencil Preparation

* A stencil (design) is prepared.
* Non-printing areas are blocked, leaving only the required design open.

---

### Step 3 – Ink Application

* Printing ink is poured onto the top of the screen.

---

### Step 4 – Squeegee Movement

* A rubber **squeegee** is moved across the screen with pressure.
* The ink is forced through the open mesh.

---

### Step 5 – Printing

* Ink passes through the stencil openings and is deposited onto the substrate.
* The required image or text is printed.

---

### Step 6 – Drying/Curing

* The printed material is dried using hot air, UV light, or an oven.
* This improves ink adhesion and durability.

---

### Step 7 – Inspection

* The printed product is inspected for print quality, colour accuracy, and defects.

---

# Advantages

* Simple and economical process
* Low equipment cost
* Thick ink layer gives bright colours
* Suitable for flat and curved surfaces
* Can print on different materials
* Good durability of printed image

---

# Disadvantages

* Slow compared to gravure and flexography
* Not suitable for very high-speed production
* Limited fine detail
* Manual operation may reduce productivity

---

# Applications

* Plastic bottles
* Glass bottles
* Food containers
* Labels and stickers
* Printed cartons
* T-shirts
* Electronic panels
* Promotional products

---

# Conclusion

Screen printing is a simple, versatile, and cost-effective printing method. It produces high-quality prints on various materials and is widely used in the packaging industry for labels, containers, cartons, and promotional products.

# Introduction

**Heat sealing** is the process of joining **two thermoplastic materials** by applying **heat and pressure**. The heat softens or melts the plastic surfaces, and pressure joins them together. After cooling, a strong and permanent seal is formed.

Heat sealing is one of the most commonly used methods for sealing **plastic pouches, sachets, and flexible food packages**.

---

# Principle

The process works on the principle that **thermoplastic materials soften when heated**. When two heated surfaces are pressed together and allowed to cool, they fuse into a strong, leak-proof seal.

---

# Heat Sealing Process

### Step 1 – Positioning

* Two plastic films are placed between the heated sealing jaws.

---

### Step 2 – Heating

* The heated jaws soften or melt the thermoplastic surfaces.

---

### Step 3 – Pressure Application

* Pressure is applied to bond the softened layers together.

---

### Step 4 – Cooling

* The sealed area is cooled while maintaining pressure.
* Cooling solidifies the plastic and forms a permanent joint.

---

### Step 5 – Finished Seal

* A strong, airtight, and leak-proof seal is obtained.

---

# Advantages

* Strong and permanent seal
* Leak-proof packaging
* Hygienic sealing
* High production speed
* Low maintenance
* Suitable for automatic machines
* Increases shelf life of food products

---

# Disadvantages

* Suitable only for thermoplastic materials
* Improper temperature may cause weak seals or film damage
* Requires controlled heating and pressure

---

# Applications

* Milk pouches
* Snack packets
* Biscuit wrappers
* Vacuum packaging
* Frozen food pouches
* Pharmaceutical sachets
* Juice pouches

---

# Conclusion

Heat sealing is an efficient and reliable sealing method used in the packaging industry. It provides strong, hygienic, and leak-proof seals that help preserve food quality and extend shelf life.

# Introduction

Printing is an important process in the packaging industry because it provides **product identification, branding, instructions, barcodes, expiry dates, and attractive designs**.

Two important printing methods are:

1. **Gravure Printing**
2. **Letterpress Printing**

These methods are widely used for printing on **paper, plastic films, cartons, labels, and flexible packaging materials**.

---

# 1. Gravure Printing

## Definition

**Gravure printing** is an **intaglio printing process** in which the **image is engraved below the surface of a metal cylinder**. The engraved cells hold the ink, which is transferred onto the packaging material under pressure. 

---

## Principle

The printing image is engraved on the cylinder surface.

* Ink fills the engraved cells.
* Excess ink is removed by a **doctor blade**.
* Ink remaining in the engraved cells is transferred to the substrate by pressure.

---

## Working Process

### Step 1

The gravure cylinder rotates in the ink fountain.

---

### Step 2

The engraved cells are filled with ink.

---

### Step 3

The **doctor blade** removes excess ink from the cylinder surface.

---

### Step 4

The packaging material passes between the **impression roller** and the gravure cylinder.

---

### Step 5

Ink is transferred from the engraved cells to the substrate.

---

### Step 6

The printed material passes through a drying unit.

---

## Advantages

* Excellent print quality
* High-speed printing
* Long printing life
* Suitable for large-scale production
* Excellent colour reproduction
* Suitable for fine images

---

## Disadvantages

* High cylinder cost
* Expensive setup
* Not economical for small production

---

## Applications

* Snack food wrappers
* Chocolate wrappers
* Milk packets
* Flexible plastic films
* Decorative laminates
* Gift wrappers

---

# 2. Letterpress Printing

## Definition

**Letterpress printing** is a **relief printing process** in which the **raised surface of the printing plate carries the ink**. The raised image directly transfers ink to the packaging material. 

---

## Principle

* Raised portions receive ink.
* Non-printing areas remain below the surface.
* The raised image presses against the substrate to produce the print.

---

## Working Process

### Step 1

Ink is applied to the raised printing plate using rollers.

---

### Step 2

Only the raised portions receive ink.

---

### Step 3

The substrate passes between the impression cylinder and the printing plate.

---

### Step 4

Pressure transfers the ink onto the packaging material.

---

### Step 5

The printed material is dried.

---

## Advantages

* Simple process
* Good print quality
* Low equipment cost
* Suitable for labels and cartons
* Durable printing plates

---

## Disadvantages

* Limited colour quality
* Less suitable for high-resolution images
* Not suitable for complex graphics
* Slower than gravure printing

---

## Applications

* Labels
* Corrugated boxes
* Cartons
* Books
* Newspapers
* Packaging boxes

Both **gravure** and **letterpress** printing methods are widely used in the packaging industry. **Gravure printing** offers superior image quality and is suitable for high-volume flexible packaging, while **letterpress printing** is simpler, more economical, and commonly used for labels, cartons, and packaging boxes.

# Introduction

Printing plays an important role in packaging by providing **brand identity, product information, expiry dates, instructions, and attractive graphics**.

Among the commonly used printing methods are:

1. **Lithography (Offset Printing)**
2. **Silk Screen Printing (Screen Printing)**

These methods are widely used for printing on paper, plastic, glass, metal, cartons, and labels.

---

# 1. Lithography (Offset Printing)

## Definition

**Lithography** is a **planographic printing process** based on the principle that **oil and water do not mix**. In this method, the image area attracts ink while the non-image area attracts water. The image is first transferred to a **rubber blanket** and then onto the packaging material. 

---

## Principle

* Image areas are **oil-loving (oleophilic)**.
* Non-image areas are **water-loving (hydrophilic)**.
* Ink sticks only to the image areas.
* The image is transferred indirectly through a rubber blanket.

---

## Components

* Printing plate
* Dampening rollers
* Ink rollers
* Blanket cylinder
* Impression cylinder
* Packaging material

---

## Working Process

### Step 1 – Plate Preparation

The image is prepared on a flat printing plate.

---

### Step 2 – Dampening

Water is applied to the plate. Non-image areas retain water.

---

### Step 3 – Inking

Ink rollers apply ink to the plate. Ink sticks only to the image areas.

---

### Step 4 – Offset Transfer

The image is transferred from the plate to the rubber blanket cylinder.

---

### Step 5 – Printing

The blanket cylinder transfers the image onto the packaging material.

---

### Step 6 – Drying

The printed material is dried and inspected.

---

## Advantages

* Excellent print quality
* High-speed printing
* Sharp images
* Accurate colour reproduction
* Long printing plate life
* Suitable for large production

---

## Disadvantages

* High initial setup cost
* Not economical for very small batches
* Requires skilled operation

---

## Applications

* Cartons
* Labels
* Paper packaging
* Books
* Magazines
* Beverage cartons

---

### Step 5 – Printing

# Introduction

**Sealing** is the process of joining two packaging materials to produce a **strong, airtight, and leak-proof package**. It plays a vital role in maintaining food quality, preventing contamination, and extending shelf life.

The two commonly used sealing methods are:

1. **Heat Sealing**
2. **Ultrasonic Sealing**

---

# 1. Heat Sealing Process

## Definition

**Heat sealing** is the process of joining **two thermoplastic materials** by applying **heat and pressure**. The heat softens or melts the plastic surfaces, and pressure bonds them together. After cooling, a strong permanent seal is formed. 

---

## Principle

Heat sealing works on the principle that **thermoplastic materials soften when heated**. When two heated surfaces are pressed together and cooled, they fuse into a permanent joint.

---

## Components

* Upper heated jaw
* Lower heated jaw
* Thermoplastic film
* Temperature controller
* Pressure mechanism

---

## Working Process

### Step 1 – Positioning

Two plastic films are placed between the heated sealing jaws.

### Step 2 – Heating

The heated jaws soften or melt the thermoplastic surfaces.

### Step 3 – Pressure Application

Pressure is applied to fuse both layers together.

### Step 4 – Cooling

The sealed area is cooled while maintaining pressure.

### Step 5 – Finished Seal

A strong, airtight, and leak-proof seal is obtained.

---

## Advantages

* Strong sealing
* Leak-proof package
* Hygienic process
* High production speed
* Easy operation
* Low maintenance
* Extends shelf life

---

## Disadvantages

* Suitable only for thermoplastics
* Excess heat may damage the film
* Requires accurate temperature control

---

## Applications

* Milk pouches
* Snack packets
* Biscuit wrappers
* Frozen food packs
* Vacuum packaging
* Pharmaceutical sachets

# 2. Ultrasonic Sealing Process

## Definition

**Ultrasonic sealing** is a sealing process in which **high-frequency ultrasonic vibrations** generate heat at the contact surface of thermoplastic materials, causing them to melt and fuse without external heating. 

---

## Principle

Ultrasonic sealing is based on the conversion of **high-frequency electrical energy into mechanical vibrations**. These vibrations create frictional heat at the interface of the plastic layers, resulting in fusion.

---

## Components

* Ultrasonic generator
* Transducer
* Booster
* Sonotrode (Horn)
* Anvil
* Packaging material

---

## Working Process

### Step 1 – Positioning

Two plastic layers are placed between the sonotrode and anvil.

### Step 2 – Ultrasonic Vibrations

The generator supplies high-frequency electrical energy to the transducer.

### Step 3 – Heat Generation

Mechanical vibrations create frictional heat at the contact area.

### Step 4 – Pressure Application

Pressure is applied while the material softens.

### Step 5 – Cooling

The joint cools and forms a strong seal.

---

## Advantages

* No external heating required
* Very fast process
* Strong and clean seal
* Low energy consumption
* Suitable for automated production
* Minimal thermal damage
* Excellent seal quality

---

## Disadvantages

* High equipment cost
* Suitable mainly for thermoplastic materials
* Requires precise machine settings

---

## Applications

* Medical packaging
* Pharmaceutical blister packs
* Food packaging
* Plastic containers
* Electronic packaging
* Beverage cartons

# Difference Between Heat Sealing and Ultrasonic Sealing

| Feature            | Heat Sealing          | Ultrasonic Sealing                      |
| ------------------ | --------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Heat Source        | External heat         | Ultrasonic vibrations                   |
| Working Principle  | Heat + Pressure       | Frictional heat + Pressure              |
| Energy Consumption | Higher                | Lower                                   |
| Sealing Speed      | High                  | Very High                               |
| Equipment Cost     | Low                   | High                                    |
| Seal Quality       | Good                  | Excellent                               |
| Material Damage    | Possible              | Minimal                                 |
| Applications       | Food pouches, sachets | Medical, pharmaceutical, food packaging |

---

# Conclusion

Heat sealing and ultrasonic sealing are two important methods used in the packaging industry. **Heat sealing** uses external heat and pressure to join thermoplastic materials, while **ultrasonic sealing** uses high-frequency vibrations to generate frictional heat for sealing. Both methods produce strong, leak-proof seals that improve food safety, product quality, and shelf life.
